# Evaluating Research / Deep-Research Agents (2025-2026) — Research Notes

Researched 2026-09-25 via Keenable search/fetch only. All claims below are sourced from pages fetched this session; URLs and acquisition/publication dates as returned by the tool.

## (a) Key findings

### Benchmarks — report-generation / deep-research agents

1. **DeepResearch Bench (RACE + FACT)** — the foundational benchmark. "we present DeepResearch Bench, a benchmark consisting of 100 PhD-level research tasks, each meticulously crafted by domain experts across 22 distinct fields... The first is a reference-based method with adaptive criteria to assess the quality of generated research reports [RACE]. The other framework... evaluate[s] DRA's information retrieval and collection capabilities by assessing its effective citation count and overall citation accuracy [FACT]." RACE's "Pairwise Agreement Rate also surpasses the inter-agreement rate observed among human experts" (71.33% vs 68.44% human-human). Human validation cost "225 person-hours." (arxiv.org/abs/2506.11763, published 2025-06-16, acquired 2026-09-11)

2. **DeepResearch Bench II (Jan 2026 follow-up)** — moved from reference-comparison to fine-grained binary rubrics: "132 grounded research tasks across 22 domains; for each task, an agent must produce a research report that is evaluated by a set of 9,430 fine-grained binary rubrics in total, covering three dimensions: information recall, analysis, and presentation... constructed through a four-stage LLM+human pipeline... over 400 human-hours of expert review... even the strongest models satisfy fewer than 50% of the rubrics." (arxiv.org/html/2601.08536v2, acquired 2026-09-19)

3. **Dr. Bench** (2026) — explicitly designed to fix DeepResearch Bench's static-reference weakness: "214 challenging report-oriented entries across 10 broad domains... manually constructed reference bundles comprising query-specific and general-report rubrics, trustworthy sources, focus-anchor and focus-deviation keywords." Notably it also benchmarks token efficiency: "o3 and o4-mini averaged 23K and 18K tokens per report respectively, placing them lowest in contribution efficiency." (arxiv.org/abs/2510.02190, acquired 2026-09-03)

4. **ResearcherBench** — targets frontier/novel questions rather than retrievable knowledge: "65 research questions expertly selected from real-world scientific scenarios... categorized into three types: technical details, literature review, and open consulting... Our dual evaluation framework combines rubric assessment... with factual assessment, which measures citation accuracy (faithfulness) and coverage (groundedness)." Leaderboard shows faithfulness/groundedness trade sharply differ by system (OpenAI DR: 84% faithfulness / 34% groundedness; Perplexity Sonar: 62%/68%). (researcherbench.github.io, acquired 2026-09-19; arxiv.org/abs/2507.16280)

5. **ARAC-Bench** — "Researcher-Mimicking Evaluation" using accepted-paper gold standards and distilling "implicit reviewer expertise into stage-calibrated, quantifiable rubrics" across Proposal/Experiment/Synthesis; "best alignment score of only 67.9 of 100... Validation against Ph.D. Candidates rankings shows a strong correlation" (0.8141). (arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12788, acquired 2026-09-20)

6. **Expert-consulting-work benchmark (VRS)** — a rigorous rubric+verifier design worth copying: "every response is checked by a suite of binary task-specific verifiers and then independently scored by a subject-matter expert on a five-criterion 0-3 rubric (Data Integrity, Analytical Rigor, Relevance & Focus, Execution Precision, Format & Deliverability)... combined into a Verifier-Rubric Score (VRS)." Prompts deliberately embed "cognitive traps... in the form of realistic human-style errors" to stop shallow pattern-matching from scoring well. ACCEPT rates (conjunctive: rubric≥2.5 AND verifier≥80%) were "uniformly low: 21.4% Gemini, 9.5% o3, 9.5% Claude" even for frontier deep-research products. (arxiv.org/abs/2605.17554, acquired 2026-09-03; arxiv.org/html/2605.17554v1 with 70-task second release)

7. **APEX-Agents / APEX-Agents 1.1** — long-horizon professional-services agent benchmark (480 tasks, IB/consulting/law), graded by "criteria... which can be graded as true or false against an agent's output" via an LLM judge (Gemini 3 Flash, thinking=low) fed the prompt + output + change-log, not the trajectory. Top scores under 25% Pass@1 in v1.0. v1.1 found and fixed a gaming exploit: **"scattergunning" — "a behavior where models provide multiple answers despite having a single answer that is well-specified... Since APEX-Agents grades using binary rubric items that only check for inclusion of correct information, models that scattergun generally gain an advantage."** Fixed via judge-model retraining (GEPA-optimized prompt) plus explicit anti-hedging system-prompt instructions. (arxiv.org/html/2601.14242, mercor.com/blog/introducing-apex-agents-1-1, acquired 2026-09-08/13)

8. **Mr.LHDR** — long-horizon multimodal deep research; introduces Dependency-Aware Checklist Score (DACS) to catch a critical measurement gap: "final-answer accuracy substantially overstates complete, dependency-consistent task success" — best system hit 43.1% overall accuracy but only 34.3% strict accuracy once intermediate dependent conclusions were checked. Also: "withholding images lowers DACS by 12.6 points," and accuracy "decreases monotonically with chain length." (arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11318v1, acquired 2026-09-12)

9. **DRACO (Perplexity, Feb 2026)** — key finding for research-council's core value proposition: **"orchestration beats the bare model" — Perplexity Deep Research (built on Opus 4.5/4.6) substantially outperforms bare Claude Opus with web_search + code_execution tools, 70.5% vs 59.8% normalized.** Also found factual accuracy is the weak axis, and that output length doesn't track quality: "OpenAI o3 and Gemini produced the most output... yet scored mid-pack — verbosity did not buy quality." (howardism.dev/articles/deep-research-agents, acquired 2026-08-24 — secondary summary; underlying DRACO paper not independently fetched this session, flag as second-hand)

10. **From Intent to Evidence (categorical/structural benchmark)** — a formal, gaming-resistant benchmark design: 296 questions stress-testing "sequential connectivity chains," "V-structure pullbacks," "topological ordering," and "ontological falsification via the Yoneda Probe." SOTA hits only 19.9% average accuracy; models "almost universally collapse on multi-hop structural synthesis" despite doing fine on simpler axes — showing aggregate benchmark scores can hide capability collapse on specific reasoning shapes. (arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25342v1.pdf, acquired 2026-08-08)

### Benchmarks — scientific research / replication / ideation

11. **ResearchBench (astrophysics reproducibility)** — "22 papers... split into a series of tasks which require agents to generate replications of the paper's core contributions... co-developed with the original authors." Frontier agents scored "just above 20%." (ml4astro.github.io ICML2025 camera-ready, acquired 2026-05-18)

12. **PaperBench** — full from-scratch ICML-paper replication, "8,316 individually gradable subtasks" via author-co-developed hierarchical rubrics + an LLM judge (SimpleJudge) calibrated on a separate benchmark (**JudgeEval, F1=0.83**). Best agent: 21.0% replication score; human PhD baseline 41.4% best@3; "o1 initially surpasses the human baseline in early hours but falls behind after roughly 24 hours" — i.e. agents front-load progress but don't sustain long-horizon strategy. (paper-graph-ui.vercel.app summary of arXiv:2504.01848, acquired 2026-07-19)

13. **MLE-bench** (OpenAI) — 75 Kaggle competitions; best setup (o1-preview+AIDE) hit medal in 16.9% of competitions (pass@1), 34.1% at pass@8; explicitly studies "the impact of contamination from pre-training" and ships a "rule violation detector and a plagiarism detector." (github.com/openai/mle-bench, arxiv.org/pdf/2410.07095, acquired 2026-09-15/21)

14. **ScienceAgentBench** — 102 tasks from 44 peer-reviewed papers across 4 disciplines, output unified as a runnable Python program with rubric-based graded evaluation (VER/SR/CodeBERTScore/Cost) plus **two explicit anti-contamination strategies** baked into design. Best agent (Claude-3.5-Sonnet + self-debug) solved 34.3% with expert knowledge; simpler self-debug beat a complex agent framework "while costing 17 times less" — evidence that harness complexity ≠ score. (arxiv.org/abs/2410.05080, acquired 2026-09-03)

15. **SciReplicate-Bench** — 100 algorithm-reproduction tasks from 36 2024 NLP papers, "deliberately chosen [2024 papers] to minimize the risk of data leakage." Introduces **reasoning graph accuracy** (structural similarity between generated and reference reasoning graphs derived from code comments) as a way to separate "did it understand the algorithm" from "did it implement it correctly" — models scored 0.716+ on comprehension but only 0.39 execution accuracy, a large understand/execute gap. (arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00255v2, acquired 2026-08-08)

16. **MLReplicate** — end-to-end manuscript-generation benchmark from ICML 2025 outstanding papers, run through automated conference-style review + structured human review. Sobering finding on automated review: **"59% of accepted automated reviews contained fabricated or unsupported claims"** — i.e. an LLM-judge review pipeline let hallucinated papers through at high rate. Also: "neither token budget nor computational cost predicts output quality: the cheapest system outperforms the most resource-intensive system." (arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16616v1, acquired 2026-07-10)

17. **Humanity's Last Exam (HLE)** — general capability ceiling-check (2,500 expert Q&A). Heavily contested reliability: FutureHouse audit found "29%±3.7%... of the 321 text-only chemistry/biology answers were directly contradicted by peer-reviewed literature"; HLE's own creators replicated "~18% problem rate"; **HLE-Verified** re-audit: "668 items verified clean, 1,143 revised, 689 left flagged... re-scoring eight frontier models on the cleaned version moved average accuracy by 7-10 points overall and by 30-40 points specifically on the items that had been flawed." Also not yet saturated: top score ~53% as of mid-2026 vs 90% "saturation" line used for MMLU-era benchmarks. (themodelgap.com/benchmarks/hle, arxiv.org/html/2602.13964v4, capitalandcompute.net, all acquired 2026-08/09)

18. **Hypothesis-quality / ideation benchmarks**:
    - **HypoBench**: "7 real-world tasks and 5 synthetic tasks with 194 distinct datasets... best models and methods only recovering 38.8% of the ground-truth hypotheses" as difficulty scales; argues explanatory power (predictive utility on held-out data) should be first-order, novelty/plausibility/clarity secondary and judged by an LLM (GPT-4o). (arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11524, acquired 2026-09-23)
    - **ResearchBench (inspiration-decomposition)** (Liu et al.): decomposes discovery into inspiration retrieval / hypothesis composition / hypothesis ranking across 1,386 papers, 12 disciplines; **contamination control by construction**: "we focus exclusively on papers published in 2024, ensuring minimal overlap with LLM pretraining data... the framework can automatically extract more recent papers to avoid overlapping" as model cutoffs advance. (arxiv.org/abs/2503.21248v1, acquired 2026-09-12)
    - **AgentIdeaBench**: static vs "active exploration" (agent does its own literature retrieval rather than being fed curated papers) — "Active exploration reveals considerably more capability headroom... Performance scales about twice as fast... The gain reflects better grounding... while leaving measured originality unchanged." Implication: static ideation benchmarks understate/mismeasure real capability. (arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07611, acquired 2026-09-09)
    - **Creativity metrics paper**: decomposes novelty into Exact-Match P-Novelty, Variable-level P-Novelty, and H-Novelty; finds "Variable-level P-Novelty is the creativity dimension most strongly associated with research performance" — a concrete, cheap proxy metric for "is this actually a different idea" vs "same idea, different wording." (arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14057v1, acquired 2026-09-16)
    - **SCI-IDEA**: important warning on LLM-judge vs human divergence for ideation — "LLM-based scores exceed expert ratings by 3–4 points on a 10-point scale, with near-zero inter-rater correlation (r = 0.02–0.17, p > 0.05)." LLM judges of idea quality may be measuring something almost uncorrelated with expert judgment. (link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-026-07036-8, acquired 2026-09-15)

### Citation accuracy / coverage / groundedness measurement

19. **DeepResearch Bench's FACT framework**: extracts statements+citations, retrieves the actual cited page, and uses an LLM judge to check support; validated at "96%... support... and 92% not-support" agreement with humans using Gemini-2.5-Flash. Result: citation volume and accuracy trade off (Gemini: 111 citations/task at 81% accuracy; Perplexity: 31 citations/task at 90% accuracy).

20. **DeepTRACE** audit framework: formalizes **Citation Accuracy** = (accurate citations)/(all citations) and **Citation Thoroughness** = (accurate citations found)/(all possible accurate citations), computed from citation × factual-support matrices; found across GPT-4.5/5, You.com, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini that "citation accuracy rang[es] from 40–80% across systems" and deep-research configs "remain highly one-sided on debate queries." (arxiv.org/html/2509.04499v1, acquired 2026-09-13)

21. **"On-Device Deep Research at 4B"** formalizes three metrics teams should separate rather than blend: **faithfulness** (does the cited source support the claim), **citation recall/coverage** (did we cite the gold evidence that exists), and **trustworthy coverage** = faithfulness × recall — "the one number to compare across arms." Key trap: "a naive arm can post high faithfulness while citing the wrong sources" — faithfulness alone is gameable by citing whatever you happened to retrieve. Practical lever: "Raising exposure from 400 to 1500 characters per source lifts faithfulness from 0.45 to 0.58" — nearly free (≈235 extra output tokens) — while "trustworthy coverage stays near 0.22... because citation recall is held near 0.40 by the retrieval stage," i.e. retrieval quality, not writing quality, is usually the bottleneck. (arxiv.org/abs/2607.12257, acquired 2026-09-10)

22. **Who is the Agent to Blame?** — localizes citation/faithfulness errors to specific agents in a multi-agent DR pipeline rather than scoring only the final report: "84.7% of final-report errors in AI-Q originate at the orchestrator, roughly 31% of them hallucinations and the rest citation mistakes." Four-type taxonomy: hallucination, uncited-input-reliance, uncited-output, insufficient-citations. Two simple interventions raised citation recall by 5% once the faulty agent was identified. (arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24306v1, acquired 2026-08-27)

23. **ClaimProbe / ClaimWriter**: claim-level (not report-level) auditing — decomposes reports into claims and scores Hallucination, Misattribution, Citation hygiene (Uncited-but-supported), and Necessary-fact Recall separately. Key insight: **"holistic quality and source faithfulness can diverge... A writer can keep the holistic evaluation score close to the baseline while changing the claim-to-source relationship substantially"** — i.e. rubric/RACE-style scores can stay flat while real grounding gets worse or better. (arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28643, acquired 2026-09-10)

24. **Citation Reliability test protocol** (practitioner piece, useful methodology reference): four independently-testable dimensions — existence, bibliographic accuracy, claim support, source relevance — each checkable against free public APIs (Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, NCBI E-utilities). Notes verifier-strictness sensitivity: "the measured unsupported-citation rate swings from about 3% to about 18% on identical model output depending only on the strictness of the automated verifier." Also flags that vendor claims about "never hallucinated" citations lack independent audits in most cases. (intuitionlabs.ai/articles/citation-reliability-ai-literature-reviews, acquired 2026-09-11)

### LLM-as-judge reliability

25. **LongJudgeBench**: meta-evaluation of judges on long-form (avg 9,249 tokens) outputs. Headline number: **"mean accuracy was 0.5627, which is only slightly better than a random guess (0.5) in a binary preference task"** across 32 model/setting combos. Failure modes named: "Superficial Coverage Bias" (long, well-formatted docs score high even off-topic), "Concept Misgrounding" in technical domains, and severe **position bias** — "GPT-4o-mini showed inconsistency rates as high as 78.7% when the order of the same two responses was swapped." (arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01629v3, acquired 2026-09-04)

26. **REFLECT** — controlled-intervention meta-benchmark specific to deep-research agents/reports: "even the best-performing models achieve overall accuracies below 55% across reasoning, tool-use, and report-quality failures, with especially poor performance on evidence verification" (45.7% reasoning, 54.5% tool-use, 47.5% report-quality). "groundedness" and "faithfulness" failures are the hardest for judges to catch. (arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19196, acquired 2026-05-20)

27. **RUVER-BENCH** — rubric-*verification* reliability specifically (given one rubric item + output, can the judge say pass/fail correctly): "Deep Research reports averaging 7.1K tokens... substantial noise remains even for the best-performing models... models perform worse on Agentic Coding... batching multiple rubrics improves efficiency but introduces a clear trade-off with verification accuracy, while majority voting improves reliability with diminishing returns." (arxiv.org/abs/2606.29920, acquired 2026-09-04)

28. **"When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement"**: judge upgrades are "not interchangeable" — swapping judges can move scores for reasons unrelated to underlying quality ("evaluator-replacement ambiguity"). Even strong judges retain real bias: "MiniMax-M2.7 still changes 14.7% of verdicts under A/B reversal." Prescribes reporting "dataset slices, bias probes, error-dependence estimates, and protocol audit trails" alongside any LLM-judge score. (arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08535v1, acquired 2026-07-11)

29. **Deep-Research Eval (page-level auditing)**: motivates why whole-report LLM judging is unreliable — "attention dilution" and "lost-in-the-middle" on long documents — and proposes splitting the report into pages before judging plus a "Paged-RAG" fact-check step; reaches "Composite Consistency Index (CCI) of 0.7585, an absolute increase of 0.4588 over baselines" vs whole-document judging. (mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/5/2546, acquired 2026-09-04)

30. **"Just Ask the Model"** (encouraging counter-point): a single-prompt, no-iteration, no-RAG LLM judge (GPT-5 Pro) scoring real economics papers against the same rubric human reviewers use showed **"rank agreement with the human panel on overall quality is statistically indistinguishable from pairwise human inter-rater agreement"** — but with "consistent failure modes: compressed rating scales, uneven criterion coverage." Net: a cheap one-shot LLM judge can be about as good as *adding one more human rater*, not a replacement for the panel, and central-tendency compression is a real, correctable bias. (llm-uj-research-eval.netlify.app / valentinklotzbuecher.github.io, acquired 2026-07/09)

### Contamination

31. **Search-Time Contamination (STC)** — specific to *agents that browse during eval*, distinct from training-data contamination: "an agent may retrieve public benchmark metadata, question context, or even ground-truth answers via web search." Three severities defined (metadata leakage, question-context leakage, explicit answer leakage). Measured: "STC is widespread and can inflate performance by up to 4%," and on MedMCQA "nearly one quarter of the questions can have their answers retrieved from the web." Gemini Deep Research was observed visiting pages containing both question and answer "for 60% of the 100 sampled MedQA questions." (arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05241v1.pdf, acquired 2026-06-09)

32. **"Benchmark Contamination: A Taxonomy Organized by Defeated Mitigation"** — the most rigorous framework found; five contamination types (Direct, Derivative, Temporal, Distributional, **Acquired**), where held-out test sets only fix Type 1. Type 5 ("Acquired," i.e. runtime retrieval/tool contamination) is exactly research-council's exposure surface as a live web-browsing agent, with sub-levels: 5a environmental (secrets sitting in the eval sandbox), 5b retrieval (agent searches and finds the benchmark/labels online — same as STC above), 5c adversarial (agent escapes sandbox). "Acquired contamination is a property of a run: it cannot be certified at benchmark release, and the party publishing the score is the party positioned to disclose it." (arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29463, acquired 2026-09-04)

33. **Counter-evidence that contamination doesn't always matter for rankings**: "the rank correlation between a standard leaderboard and a paraphrase-controlled leaderboard is 0.997... contamination among these public models is therefore largely uniform: it inflates absolute scores without reordering the leaderboard" — i.e. contamination is a bigger threat to *absolute* score claims ("we hit 80%!") than to *relative* comparisons (agent A beats agent B), unless contamination is differential across the two systems being compared. (arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02899v1, acquired 2026-09-05)

### Human expert review protocols

34. **Human evaluation design guide (Koji)**: concrete, actionable protocol — write rubric with concrete anchors before looking at outputs; decompose Likert items into binary sub-criteria (materially improves agreement); freeze a 15-25 item calibration set to train every new rater; 2+ raters minimum (3 for subjective criteria); target **Cohen's kappa ≥ 0.7** (below 0.4 = rewrite rubric, 0.6-0.8 acceptable, >0.8 = safe to consider partial automation); blind the source, randomize order, counterbalance pairs, insert known-answer items to catch rater drift, re-bench quarterly. Sample size: "150–300 stratified real inputs" for a ship/no-ship gate. (koji.so/docs/human-evaluation-ai-outputs, acquired 2026-08-03)

35. **EnterpriseVal**: enterprise-grade protocol combining blinded expert grading with prediction-powered inference to stretch scarce expert time — judge scores everything, humans score a random subset, and "the reported estimate is the prediction-powered estimator." Hard rule: **"A system's self-assessment is never a gated metric: it violates blinding and inherits judge self-preference."** Requires agreement (κ) above a threshold before a metric is allowed into a ship/no-ship gate — "otherwise the rubric, not the system, is revised." (arxiv.org/html/2609.21841, acquired 2026-09-21)

### Cheap internal eval harnesses (practitioner-level, directly actionable)

36. **General pattern across multiple sources (pytest harnesses, n8n golden-set pattern, "eval-harness-first" skill)**: consistent recipe —
    - Golden set: **20-150 labeled cases** to start (real production traces > synthetic), each with input, expected tool calls/behavior, and either a rubric or reference; include "must-refuse"/must-escalate negative cases and adversarial/edge cases (~50% happy path, ~25% edge case, ~10% must-refuse, ~15% adversarial per one practitioner rule of thumb).
    - **Layered scoring**: deterministic/structured checks first (schema validation, forbidden/required tool calls, exact-match where possible — free, zero-variance), LLM-judge only for genuinely subjective criteria, small human-review queue for disputes/calibration.
    - **Freeze a holdout**: "Hold back 20 to 30 cases that you never look at during development... the holdout is what tells you how much of the improvement was real."
    - **Baseline file is a first-class artifact**: `eval/baseline-<model>.json` — every later checkpoint/version is compared against it, not against nothing.
    - **CI gate**: run cheap subset on every change, full suite nightly/on model or prompt change; fail build on regression beyond a tolerance (e.g. >2-3 point drop), not on any deviation (LLM outputs are a distribution, run 3-5x and compare aggregate, not single-run equality).
    - **Golden set must be versioned and must rot on purpose**: rotate 10-20% monthly, add every real production failure the same day it's found ("the failure becomes a regression test, and the same bug cannot escape twice").
    (kusuma.dev/en/blog/agent-evals-pytest-harness; cheatcoders.net/ai-bootcamp-day-009-eval-harness-day-one; medium.rebeauty-writing.com [n8n]; mdskill.dev/wshobson/agents/eval-harness-first; kunalganglani.com/blog/agent-evaluation-harness-replay; appforge.hu/en/blog/measuring-ai-agents-eval; alatirok.com/build-ai-agent-eval-pipeline-pytest — all acquired 2026-06 through 2026-09)

37. **A/B vs baseline agent at equal budget — closest direct evidence found**: no single source in this session ran "research-council vs plain-model-at-equal-token-budget" as its explicit design, but three findings compose into exactly that test: (i) DRACO's harness-vs-bare-model comparison methodology (orchestrated system vs bare model + same tools, normalized score) is the right shape; (ii) ScienceAgentBench's finding that a *simpler* agent (self-debug) beat a more complex one (OpenHands CodeAct) "while costing 17 times less" shows budget-normalized comparison can invert naive "more scaffolding = better" assumptions; (iii) "Not Worth Another Token" (marginal-value-estimation paper, cited inside the howardism.dev synthesis) explicitly frames deep-research agent quality as a pruning/marginal-value problem holding the pipeline fixed and varying only spend — the right frame for "is research-council worth its extra tokens over a plain model at the same budget."

## (b) Counter-evidence / caveats

- **LLM judges are measurably unreliable for exactly the artifact class research-council produces** (long-form reports with citations): LongJudgeBench's 0.56 mean accuracy (near chance) and REFLECT's <55% judge accuracy on report-quality/evidence-verification are hard ceilings on how much any RACE/FACT-style automated eval can be trusted without human spot-checks. Any internal harness that leans entirely on LLM-judge scoring inherits this ceiling.
- **Rubric benchmarks can still be gamed**: APEX-Agents' "scattergunning" discovery (models hedge with every plausible answer to inflate binary-rubric pass rates) is a direct warning for any FINDINGS.md/HANDOFF.md rubric that grades "did the report mention X" as binary pass/fail — an agent optimizing against that rubric (even implicitly, via RLHF-shaped over-hedging) can win by covering every possibility rather than committing to the best-supported one.
- **Automated review-of-review can itself hallucinate approval**: MLReplicate found 59% of *accepted* automated conference-style reviews contained fabricated/unsupported claims — a sharp warning against using an LLM-judge "peer review" step as the sole quality gate for research-council output.
- **Faithfulness metrics are gameable in isolation**: "a naive arm can post high faithfulness while citing the wrong sources" (On-Device Deep Research paper) — a lone faithfulness number without paired recall/coverage can look great while citing irrelevant-but-technically-supportive sources.
- **Contamination doesn't necessarily change which agent looks better**, only how high scores look in absolute terms (rank-correlation 0.997 finding) — so an internal eval focused on *relative* comparison (research-council vs plain model) is less contamination-fragile than one chasing an absolute leaderboard number, **provided** both arms face identical, current, non-benchmark-derived tasks (search-time contamination could still differentially help the browsing-capable arm).
- **HLE and other headline benchmarks are noisier than commonly assumed** (18-29% flawed-item rates found by independent audits) — a reminder not to borrow a "hard eval" off the shelf as ground truth without checking its own error rate first.
- **Ideation/hypothesis LLM-judge scores can be almost uncorrelated with expert judgment** (SCI-IDEA: r=0.02-0.17) even while looking internally consistent — a serious caution against using an LLM judge to score "how good was this research idea" without an expert-calibration pass.

## (c) Concrete upgrade ideas for research-council (tied to evidence)

1. **[HIGH] Build a small internal eval harness now, in the golden-set + CI-gate pattern, rather than waiting for a "real" benchmark.** Concretely: 20-40 real past research-council tasks (the ones that already have FINDINGS.md/HANDOFF.md output) become the golden set; write a rubric per task type derived from the actual FINDINGS.md/HANDOFF.md structure (facts found, citations, contradictions surfaced, actionable recommendations); score with a **layered** approach — deterministic checks first (did it produce both files, does every claim in FINDINGS.md have a URL+date, word count / structure compliance), then LLM-judge only for subjective quality dimensions, with a frozen 20-30% holdout never used to tune prompts. This directly operationalizes finding #36 and sidesteps the field's single biggest known failure mode (judge-only scoring). *Impact: high — this is literally the missing piece named in the task ("nobody has measured whether it produces better research than a plain model").*

2. **[HIGH] Adopt a DRACO-style paired comparison: research-council vs. the same base model with the same tools but no orchestration, at matched token/wall-clock budget.** This is the single most direct answer to "does research-council beat a plain model." Report the comparison the way ScienceAgentBench and "Not Worth Another Token" frame it — quality per token spent, not quality alone — since finding #37/#14 both show a simpler/cheaper approach can beat a more elaborate scaffold. *Impact: high — directly tests the stated gap; cheap to run (no new benchmark construction needed, just an A/B harness around existing tasks).*

3. **[HIGH] Split "did it cite something" from "was the citation right" from "did it cite everything it should have," exactly as faithfulness/recall/trustworthy-coverage (#21) and citation-accuracy/thoroughness (#20) do.** A single "citations look present" check is the weakest possible signal and is explicitly shown to be gameable (findings #21, #23). Minimum viable version: for every claim with a citation, (a) fetch the cited URL, (b) LLM-judge whether it supports the claim (faithfulness), (c) separately track what fraction of claims are cited at all (coverage/uncited rate). This is cheap — a handful of fetches plus one judge call per claim — and catches the exact failure mode (confidently citing the wrong thing) that plain rubric scores miss.

4. **[MED] Add adversarial "cognitive trap" cases to the golden set, following the expert-consulting-work benchmark (#6) and the MisKnow-Agent poisoning result (from the DRACO/adjacent synthesis: "one plausible-but-false document... raises false-conclusion adoption from 0% to 54.7%").** Concretely: seed 2-3 golden-set tasks with a deliberately misleading-but-plausible source in the likely search results (or a known contradiction between two sources) and check whether research-council's FINDINGS.md flags the contradiction rather than picking one side uncritically. This is a much stronger test of "is this better than a plain model" than average-case tasks, since plain single-pass models are exactly the ones shown to adopt bad evidence uncritically.

5. **[MED] Guard against LLM-judge unreliability by never using a single-pass, single-judge score as a ship/no-ship gate.** Concretely: (a) always pair automated judge scores with periodic human spot-checks on a small sample (10-20%), per the EnterpriseVal prediction-powered-inference pattern (#35) and the Koji kappa-gate pattern (#34: kappa ≥0.7 before trusting a rubric); (b) if using an LLM judge, prefer fine-grained binary rubric verification per claim/section over holistic 1-10 scoring, since RUVER-BENCH and DeepResearch-Bench-II both show binary/rubric decomposition is more reliable than holistic scoring; (c) randomize which arm (research-council vs. plain model) is shown first/labeled which, to control the position/self-preference bias documented in LongJudgeBench and "When the Judge Changes."

6. **[MED] Treat "hypothesis/insight quality" as a separate axis from "report completeness," and don't trust an off-the-shelf LLM-judge novelty score alone.** If research-council's differentiator is supposed to be better research judgment (not just more citations), borrow the Variable-level P-Novelty idea (#18c) — track whether recommendations touch variables/considerations a plain single-pass model didn't even raise, not just whether the wording differs — and cross-check any LLM-judge novelty/insight score against at least a handful of human ratings given SCI-IDEA's near-zero LLM/human correlation finding (#18d, #general caveat).

7. **[LOW-MED] Contamination-proof the eval task set by construction, not by hoping.** Use only recent, obscure-enough topics (or company-internal topics with no public ground truth) for golden-set tasks, following the pattern used by ResearchBench (2024-only papers) and ScienceAgentBench (explicit anti-contamination design). Since research-council actively browses the web, Search-Time-Contamination (#31) is a live risk if eval tasks are drawn from any public, well-indexed benchmark — the agent could retrieve the eval's own answer key. Given finding #33 (contamination mostly inflates absolute scores, not rankings), this matters most if the plain-model baseline and research-council differ in browsing depth/capability — which they will by design — so STC could differentially help the browsing-heavier arm.

8. **[LOW] Borrow the "auto-reject on any zero criterion" idea from the VRS scheme (#6)** for HANDOFF.md quality: define a small number of hard, binary must-pass gates (e.g., "every major claim has a source," "no unresolved contradictions in FINDINGS.md," "HANDOFF.md's recommended next step is executable, not vague") and report both a continuous quality score and a strict pass/fail, since the VRS paper shows these two views can rank agents in opposite orders and the strict one is usually the more decision-relevant one.

### (1) DRACO — exact protocol and sample size

Found DRACO's actual paper (Round 1 only had it via a secondary blog synthesis): **arxiv.org/abs/2602.11685**, "DRACO: a Cross-Domain Benchmark for Deep Research Accuracy, Completeness, and Objectivity" (Zhong, Zhang, Southern, Yang [Harvard], Wang, Jung, Zhang, Yarats, Ho, Ma — Perplexity + Harvard, submitted 2026-02-12, verified resolves, acquired 2026-09-19). Exact protocol as stated in the paper:

- **Task sourcing**: 100 tasks, not synthetic — "sampled from a de-identified dataset of Perplexity Deep Research requests," specifically drawn from fall-2025 production queries where users had expressed dissatisfaction (i.e. deliberately sourced from hard, real failure cases), then anonymized/augmented/filtered for objectivity and PII removal. Spans "10 general and specialized domains" and sources from "40 countries."
- **Rubric construction (the closest thing to a "how to build golden answers" protocol found for a professional-grade benchmark)**: "Twenty-six domain experts, including medical professionals, attorneys, financial analysts, software engineers, and designers" built rubrics through a **four-stage pipeline**: (i) one expert drafts the rubric, (ii) a second expert reviews and refines it, (iii) a **"saturation test"** — the current best system must NOT be able to score above ~90% on it, which functions as a difficulty/leakage check (if a system already aces the rubric, the task or rubric is too easy/lenient and gets reworked), (iv) two more experts (3rd and 4th) validate for quality. Each task ends up with "an average of 39 criteria" spanning 4 dimensions (factual accuracy, breadth/depth of analysis, presentation quality, citation quality).
- **Grading protocol / sample size for statistical stability**: each of the 100 tasks is "evaluated over **5 independent LLM-as-a-judge grading runs**," with standard deviation reported per system across those 5 runs (e.g. Perplexity DR/Opus 4.6: 70.5% ± 0.3; Gemini DR: 59.0% ± 0.4; Claude Opus 4.6 bare+tools: 59.8% ± 0.3). The tight SDs (±0.2-0.4 points on a 0-100 scale) suggest 5 judge-runs per task is enough to get a stable per-system mean at n=100 tasks — this is the most concrete "how many judge repeats do you need" number found in this session.
- **The paired-comparison finding cited in Round 1, confirmed at source**: "Perplexity Deep Research substantially outperforms Opus 4.5 and 4.6 with web search and code execution, indicating the importance of agent orchestration beyond the base model" — 70.5% (orchestrated, Opus 4.6 base) vs 59.8% (bare Opus 4.6 + web_search + code_execution, same base model family). This is a same-base-model, tools-held-constant, orchestration-only delta of **+10.7 points**, which is exactly the shape of comparison research-council needs to run against a plain model.
- **On statistical significance for a solo dev replicating this**: DRACO does not report a formal significance test (e.g. t-test/bootstrap CI) between systems in what was retrieved this session — it relies on the 5-run SD as an implicit stability check. For a much smaller golden set (the 20-40 tasks proposed in Round 1's idea #1), the practical takeaway is: run **each task through both arms 3-5 times** (not once), report mean ± SD per arm the same way DRACO does, and treat a gap smaller than roughly 2× the combined SD as noise — this mirrors the "2-point noise floor" convention independently seen on HLE (themodelgap.com, Round 1 source) and is a cheap, defensible substitute for a full significance test when n is small.

### (2) Cheapest credible eval for a solo developer — task count, metrics, incident-derived goldens

Consistent, convergent guidance across five independent practitioner sources found this round:

- **Starting size**: Anthropic's own internal guidance (as summarized by a practitioner source) is "**twenty to fifty simple tasks drawn from real failures** is a credible starting point, not a placeholder to be embarrassed about, because early in a suite's life each change tends to have a large, clearly visible effect, so a small sample is still informative." (absolutedigitalpublishers.com/articles/building-a-custom-evaluation-suite-for-a-production-agent, acquired 2026-08-21 — secondary summary of Anthropic guidance, not Anthropic's own page fetched directly this session). A second independent source gives near-identical numbers from direct experience: "40 cases against a live model took about 90 seconds and roughly 25 cents per run" (drpranayjha.com, acquired 2026-07-23). A third gives a hard ceiling for when judge cost dominates: "**Around a thousand cases per route is the practical ceiling, and the constraint is judge cost rather than statistics**... Sixty rubrics against a thousand cases is sixty thousand calls per run." (agentshonestly.com/book/evals/datasets, acquired 2026-09-07).
- **Where golden answers come from when you have no benchmark budget — build from debugging/incident history with a known root cause, exactly as the coordinator asked**: three sources converge on the same mechanism, one with a ready-made schema:
  - "Manually authored evals reflect what developers think will go wrong; production incidents reveal what actually goes wrong. Each incident is a candidate regression case." — with a concrete extraction template: `incident_id, date, source, failure_mode, input.payload, actual_output, expected_output, acceptance_criteria, grader, severity, root_cause, evict_when`. Sources to mine: `postmortems/*.md`, `gh issue list --label bug,incident,regression`, error-monitoring configs. (agentpatterns.ai/agent-readiness/bootstrap-incident-to-eval, acquired 2026-05-20)
  - "The rule I hold: every production incident ends with a new eval case, not just a fix. The prompt patch closes today's bug. The case is what keeps it closed." Sourcing priority: production traces (filter for wrong/thin/refused-when-shouldn't outputs) > support tickets/complaints (the exact failing input + correct behavior is "a finished eval case handed to you for free") > invented cases only to pre-fill gaps before real traffic exists, explicitly marked synthetic and replaced once real data arrives. (mattgoren.com/ai/building-eval-datasets, acquired 2026-08-05)
  - Root-cause-specific framing: "An eval case pulled from an incident log is functionally a postmortem action item that has been converted into a regression test instead of a paragraph of prose," citing Google SRE practice of aggregating postmortems across teams "specifically to find common themes rather than treating each incident as an isolated event... the same aggregation is what turns twenty individually interesting agent failures into a suite that covers a genuine failure taxonomy." (absolutedigitalpublishers.com, same source as above)
- **What to store per case — assertions, not reference text**: "Storing a full reference answer sounds rigorous and is a trap, because there are twenty acceptable phrasings of any correct reply and string comparison against one of them fails all nineteen others. What you want instead are assertions: things that must appear, things that must never appear, and the source that should have been cited." (drpranayjha.com) — directly actionable for research-council's FINDINGS.md/HANDOFF.md: grade "does it cite the source X" / "does it flag contradiction Y" rather than exact text match.
- **Metrics for a solo-dev harness (converging list across sources)**: task/case success rate against assertions, a frozen holdout (Round 1 already had this), label-agreement kappa between two people writing the same case ("Two annotators on the same guidance should agree; below about 0.7 kappa the labels are noise... A disagreeing pair is a spec bug, not an annotator problem" — agentshonestly.com), and a cheap/expensive two-tier CI split: free deterministic/replay checks on every PR, a nightly live-model run against the golden set with pass-rate tracked over time (totalshiftleft.ai/blog/llm-evals-guide, drpranayjha.com — both acquired 2026-07/08 2026).
- **Coverage discipline (four-bucket model)**: "A serious golden set is not one pile. It is four... Stratified production sample / Adversarial library / Constructed edge cases / Shipped-failure replays... The fourth is the one that grows itself." Plus a sharp warning against synthetic-only sets: "Synthetic data collapses toward the modal case... Never generate eval data with the model family you are evaluating. A suspiciously high synthetic score is a suspicion about the set first." (agentshonestly.com/book/evals/datasets)

### (3) Cognitive-trap / planted-false-source eval designs — deeper detail

Round 1 already found the two best sources; this round pulled the full mechanics:

- **MisKnow-Agent** (verified: arxiv.org/abs/2607.20891, "Is Deep Research Reliable? Misleading Knowledge Induces False Conclusions," submitted 2026-07-23, Shanghai AI Laboratory + Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications). Exact construction recipe, useful as a template to steal:
  1. **Blueprint**: for a given research task, author a canonical false conclusion plus a pool of fictional/real institution names stratified into **authority tiers** (High: e.g. "MIT Quantum Simulation Center"; Medium: e.g. regional university center; Low: e.g. an enthusiast forum) and **presentation styles** (Paper / News / Blog / Post).
  2. **Generation + filtering**: generate documents per authority×style combination, then filter through **five independent search-enabled verifier models plus 100%-coverage manual audit** until only documents unanimously classified as misleading survive — 5,933 instances survived this filter across 100 DeepResearch-Bench tasks.
  3. **Injection**: place the single planted document into the agent's actual retrieval pool (not the open web — isolated per-run pools, so nothing real gets poisoned) at controlled **lifecycle timing** (cold-start / mid-research / just-before-final-synthesis) and controlled **rank position** (front/middle/back of search results).
  4. **Metric — False-Conclusion Adoption Rate (FCAR)**: report-level, binary per report — counts only reports whose "own synthesis, conclusion, recommendation, or executive summary presents the false claim as true or best-supported." Mentioning, quoting, attributing, hedging, contrasting, or refuting the claim all count as **non-adoption** — this is a strict, gameable-resistant definition. Judge agreement with two blinded human annotators: **99.7% raw agreement, Cohen's κ = 0.993** on 300 reports (very high because the label is a sharply-specified binary, not a quality score — consistent with Round 1's general finding that binary/rubric decomposition beats holistic LLM-judge scoring).
  5. **Headline numbers directly reusable as target thresholds for a research-council red-team suite**: single-document injection raises mean FCAR from **0% (no-injection control) to 54.7%**; injecting right before final synthesis (vs. cold-start/mid-research) raises it further to **85.0-85.5%**; search-result rank position moves it by at most ~1.5-2 points (irrelevant); adding more documents (up to 5) barely moves it (46.2%→48.7%) — **one planted document is already sufficient**; high-authority sourcing beats low-authority by ~11 points, and paper-style beats post-style by ~18 points; pre-research verification prompting, post-research claim-by-claim refinement, and both combined reduce FCAR to 37-57% / 20-58% / 15-62% respectively but **never eliminate adoption**, and combining defenses can occasionally be *worse* than one defense alone (re-verification can re-retrieve and re-endorse the same poisoned document).
- **Expert-consulting-work benchmark's "cognitive traps"** (Round 1, arxiv.org/abs/2605.17554, re-confirmed resolving) — the complementary, cheaper-to-build trap design: instead of a generated adversarial document, seed **realistic human-style errors directly into the source materials** the agent is given — "inconsistent units, footnote-body contradictions, non-standard date formats" plus "deterministic precision failures (averaging where weighting is required, defaulting to plausible numbers in the absence of an explicit value)." This is much cheaper for a solo dev to build than MisKnow-Agent's 5-verifier-model pipeline: just hand-edit 2-3 of your golden-set source documents to contain a planted inconsistency, then grade pass/fail on whether the report catches it.
- **Practical synthesis for research-council**: combine both — (a) MisKnow-Agent-style: plant one high-authority-looking fake source in the likely search results for 2-3 golden tasks and score FCAR-style (did the final report adopt the false conclusion, y/n — cheap, sharp signal); (b) VRS-style: hand-edit numbers/units/dates in an existing real source to be internally inconsistent and check whether FINDINGS.md flags the contradiction. Both are far cheaper than building either paper's full pipeline and both produce a binary, low-ambiguity pass/fail rather than a fuzzy quality score.

### (4) Process metrics — did the agent change its mind on disconfirming evidence?

This sub-direction turned out to have a surprisingly rich, directly-transferable literature under the "sycophancy / belief-revision / certainty-robustness" umbrella — none of it framed for deep-research agents specifically, but the metrics transfer directly to grading whether research-council's FINDINGS.md appropriately updates when it hits contradicting evidence mid-research.

- **Certainty Robustness Benchmark** (verified: arxiv.org/abs/2603.03330, "Certainty robustness: Evaluating LLM stability under self-challenging prompts," submitted Feb 2026, acquired 2026-08-22). Directly reusable **2×2 scoring rubric** for "did it change its mind, and was it right to": for every question, cross (initial answer correct/incorrect) × (post-challenge answer correct/incorrect) → 4 cells: Correct→Correct (good stability, score 2), Correct→Incorrect (**bad flip**, score 1... actually scored as partial in their scheme — see raw scores: CC=2, CI=1, IC=1, II=0), Incorrect→Correct (**good flip** = justified self-correction), Incorrect→Incorrect (no signal). On 200 LiveBench questions across 4 frontier models: **Claude Sonnet 4.5 collapsed from 131/200 correct to 49/200 correct under explicit contradiction ("You are wrong!"), with 93 "TF" (true-to-false, i.e. bad/unjustified) flips out of 200** — an 82-point accuracy collapse — while Gemini 3 Pro stayed stable (169→166 correct, only 14 TF flips) under the same challenge. Key methodological point for research-council: **the benchmark explicitly separates "justified self-corrections" from "unjustified answer changes"** rather than scoring "did it change its answer" as inherently good or bad — this is exactly the discrimination research-council's HANDOFF.md needs (flip when the new evidence is actually better, don't flip when it's just a confident-sounding contradiction).
- **BeliefShift** (verified: arxiv.org/abs/2603.23848, acquired 2026-09-12/2026-05-22) contributes four decomposable metrics worth borrowing conceptually: **Belief Revision Accuracy (BRA)** — did the agent update correctly when genuinely new evidence arrived; **Drift Coherence Score (DCS)** — did the agent stay put when no new evidence arrived (i.e., resisted being talked out of a correct finding by mere pressure/repetition); **Contradiction Resolution Rate (CRR)** — did it explicitly flag the contradiction rather than silently overwriting; **Evidence Sensitivity Index (ESI)** — is the agent more responsive to genuine evidence than to mere social/repeated pressure (their key finding: RAG/memory access improved BRA and CRR but had "almost no effect" on DCS — i.e., "opinion drift is not a memory problem; it is a reasoning problem"). The BRA/DCS pairing is the direct machine-checkable analogue of "did it change its mind on disconfirming evidence" (BRA) vs. "did it get talked into changing its mind on nothing" (DCS) — a research-council eval could implement a cheap version of both with two golden-task variants per topic: one that plants real disconfirming evidence, one that plants a repeated-but-empty assertion with no new evidence.
- **MEDLEY-BENCH** (verified: arxiv.org/abs/2604.16009, actual title "Scale Buys Evaluation but Not Control in AI Metacognition," acquired 2026-09-23) adds a relevant negative finding: bigger/newer models are not reliably better at this — "MMS point estimates were not consistently ordered by available within-family size or generation comparisons" — so a research-council eval of this kind cannot assume a stronger backbone model automatically fixes belief-revision quality; it has to be measured directly, not inferred from model choice.
- **Named-but-not-independently-fetched this session** (mentioned inside jbarnes850.github.io, a secondary blog synthesis, acquired 2026-07-18 — flagged as second-hand, IDs not confirmed by direct fetch): SYCON-Bench (Hong et al., EMNLP 2025, multi-turn sycophancy) and TRUTH DECAY (Liu et al., 2025). The same secondary source raises a sharp methodological critique directly relevant to designing a research-council version of this test: **"These benchmarks share the same limitation. The critique never varies in quality. The challenge is always invalid. There is no condition where the model should revise... A model that always resists would score perfectly."** i.e., any belief-revision eval MUST include a condition where the disconfirming evidence is genuinely correct and the agent SHOULD flip, or the eval can't distinguish stubbornness from good judgment — directly reinforcing the Certainty-Robustness-Benchmark and BeliefShift design choice above.
- **Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index Report finding** (via secondary source stresstestingreality.substack.com, acquired 2026-07-21, not independently fetched — flagged second-hand): "tested 26 frontier models and found that sycophancy-induced hallucination rates ranged from 22% to 94%," and reframing false statements as "things the user believed" rather than third-party claims dropped GPT-4o's accuracy from 98.2% to 64.4% and DeepSeek R1 from >90% to 14.4% — suggests a research-council-specific test variant: phrase a planted false claim as if the *user* (the person who invoked research-council) already believes it, not just as a neutral source, since ownership-framing appears to be a stronger sycophancy trigger than source-framing alone.

### Round 2 upgrade ideas (additive to Round 1's 8; same impact scale)

9. **[HIGH] Steal DRACO's saturation test for golden-set construction**: before finalizing any golden task, run the current research-council version against it once; if it already scores ≥90%, the task/rubric is too easy or too lenient and must be reworked. Cheap, mechanical, and directly prevents the golden set from becoming stale flattery over time.
10. **[HIGH] Run every golden task through both arms (research-council vs. plain model) 3-5 times each, report mean±SD per arm, and only treat a gap as real if it exceeds roughly 2x the combined SD** — DRACO's actual protocol (5 runs/task, tight SDs of 0.2-0.4pp) and the independently-observed "2-point noise floor" convention on HLE both point to this as the minimum viable statistical discipline for a small-n comparison, without needing a full bootstrap/t-test pipeline.
11. **[HIGH] Build the golden set's "expected output" as assertions (must-cite X, must-flag-contradiction Y, must-not-assert Z), never as reference text to string-match** — universal convergent advice from every solo-dev eval source found this round, and it is what makes a golden case survive across prompt/model versions instead of needing constant rewriting.
12. **[MED] Add a two-condition belief-revision pair to the golden set, following Certainty-Robustness-Benchmark/BeliefShift/SYCON-Bench's shared design flaw as a cautionary template**: for at least 2-3 golden tasks, run a variant where genuinely new disconfirming evidence appears mid-research (should flip / cite the update) AND a variant where a merely repeated or authoritative-sounding but empty assertion appears (should NOT flip). Score both; a system that never changes its mind and a system that always caves both fail, and only measuring one condition can't tell them apart — this is the single most important design lesson from this sub-direction.
13. **[LOW-MED] Use the cheap "hand-edit a source for an internal inconsistency" trap (VRS-style) as the default cognitive-trap mechanism for a solo dev, and reserve the MisKnow-Agent-style generated-fake-source approach for later** — the former needs no verifier-model pipeline and produces an equally sharp binary pass/fail (did FINDINGS.md catch the inconsistency), while the latter's full rigor (5 verifier models, manual audit) is disproportionate for an internal team's harness.
