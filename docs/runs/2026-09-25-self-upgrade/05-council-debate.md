## (a) Key findings

### Debate vs. voting/self-consistency — the core empirical result
- **Majority voting, not debate, drives most of the reported gains.** "Debate or Vote: Which Yields Better Decisions in Multi-Agent Large Language Models?" (Choi, Zhu, Li — Univ. of Wisconsin–Madison, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight): "we find that Majority Voting alone accounts for most of the performance gains typically attributed to MAD... We prove that it induces a martingale over agents' belief trajectories, implying that debate alone does not improve expected correctness." (arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17536v2, acquired 2026-09-13; published 2025-10-23)
- **Independent replication of the martingale finding**: "Demystifying Multi-Agent Debate: The Role of Confidence and Diversity" confirms: "under homogeneous agents and uniform belief updates, debate preserves expected correctness and therefore cannot reliably improve outcomes." (arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19921v2, acquired 2026-07-13)
- **Systematic benchmark-level confirmation**: "Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate — We Must Rethink Evaluation and Embrace Model Heterogeneity": evaluated 5 MAD methods × 9 benchmarks × 4 base models — "MAD often fail to outperform simple single-agent baselines such as Chain-of-Thought and Self-Consistency, even when consuming significantly more inference-time computation." Model heterogeneity is called "a universal antidote to consistently improve current MAD frameworks." (arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08788v3, acquired 2026-05-29)
- **Compute-matched comparison, agentic (not just QA) tasks**: "Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets" — across Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama, Gemini 2.5 and 5 MAS architectures (sequential, subtask-parallel, parallel-roles, debate, ensemble): "SAS is the strongest default architecture for multi-hop reasoning... SAS also consumes much less thinking token than any MAS variants while achieving the same or better results." MAS only becomes competitive "when a single agent's effective context utilization is degraded." (arxiv.org/html/2604.02460, published 2026-04-06)
- **Automated MAS design specifically indicted**: "The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage" — "automatic MAS consistently underperform CoT-SC despite being up to 10x more expensive... current automated design paradigms produce architectural bloat that prioritizes superficial complexity which does not translate into functional utility." Crucially: "expert-architected MAS consistently outperforms automatically generated architectures... on this [decomposition-friendly] dataset" — i.e., hand-designed structure can still win, just not naive/auto-generated structure. (arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13003v2, published 2026-06-11)

### Conformity, sycophancy, correlated errors
- **Quantified conformity in homogeneous debate**: "The Cost of Consensus: Isolated Self-Correction Prevails Over Unguided Homogeneous Multi-Agent Debate" (10 agents × 3 rounds, Qwen2.5-7B/Llama-3.1-8B/Ministral-3-8B, GSM-Hard/MMLU-Hard): "sycophantic conformity, where agents uncritically adopt majority answers (modal adoption up to 85.5%)... contextual fragility... (vulnerability rate up to 70.0%)... consensus collapse, where plurality voting discards correct answers already present in the generation pool (oracle gap up to 32.3 percentage points)." Debate "consumes 2.1–3.4× more tokens... than self-correction for equal or lower accuracy." (dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3786335.3813137 and arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00914, published 2026-05-05)
- **Decomposing what actually causes stance change**: "Not All Flips Are Conformity: Decomposing Stance Convergence in Multi-Agent LLM Debate" — "spontaneous instability is a large baseline source of answer change (37% of agent-question pairs change under self-reflection alone)... strict conformity... is predominantly harmful (63.6% correct-to-wrong)... At the majority-vote level across all 4,900 questions, however, both self-reflection and full reasoning reach 77.4% (vs. 76.2% at Round 0). Peer influence contributes no measurable gain beyond self-reflection at the aggregate level, because harmful and beneficial conformity approximately cancel." (arxiv.org/abs/2606.00820, published 2026-06-02)
- **Sycophancy is model-trait-dependent and correctable**: "Too Polite to Disagree: Understanding Sycophancy Propagation in Multi-Agent Systems" — giving agents a peer sycophancy-ranking before discussion "reduces the influence of sycophancy-prone peers, mitigates error-cascades, and improves final discussion accuracy by an absolute 10.5%." (arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02668v1.pdf, acquired 2026-08-07)
- **Reasoning quality can silently degrade even when the answer looks stable**: "The Reasoning Trap" — "When copies of the same language model are prompted to debate, they do not produce diverse perspectives—they produce diverse phrasings of the same perspective... debate preserves 88% of baseline accuracy while [evidential-grounding score] drops 43%; majority-vote MAD... reduces [grounding] to 1.7% of baseline (p<10⁻⁶)." (arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01704.pdf, acquired 2026-05-23)

### MAS failure taxonomy (MAST)
- Cemri, Pan, Yang, et al. (UC Berkeley + collaborators), "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?" (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks Track): 1,642 annotated traces across 7 frameworks (MetaGPT, ChatDev, HyperAgent, AppWorld, AG2, Magentic-One, OpenManus); failure rates 41%–86.7%; **14 failure modes in 3 categories**: System Design Issues 41.8%, Inter-Agent Misalignment 36.9%, Task Verification 21.3% (κ=0.88 inter-annotator agreement). Quote: "failures identified by MAST often stem from system design issues, not just LLM limitations or simple prompt following, and require more than superficial fixes, thereby highlighting the need for structural MAS redesigns." (arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657v3, acquired 2026-05-09)
- **Interventions that worked, same base model**: "better role prompts → +9.4% on ChatDev; adding a high-level verifier → +15.6% on ChatDev." (per secondary summary of the same paper, agent-atlas.blackarcindustrial.com, acquired 2026-08-25 — cross-checked against the primary arxiv abstract's claim of case-study interventions)
- Top individual failure modes cited: disobey task specification (~11–16%), step repetition (~15–17%), reasoning-action mismatch (~14%), fail to ask for clarification (~12%), premature termination (~8%), no/incomplete verification (~7%), incorrect verification (~7%).

### Anthropic's own multi-agent research system (most directly relevant primary source)
- "How we built our multi-agent research system" (Anthropic Engineering, anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system, acquired 2026-09-14, originally published 2025-06-13): **"a multi-agent system with Claude Opus 4 as the lead agent and Claude Sonnet 4 subagents outperformed single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2% on our internal research eval."** But: **"token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance [in BrowseComp performance], with the number of tool calls and the model choice as the two other explanatory factors."** And on cost: **"agents typically use about 4× more tokens than chat interactions, and multi-agent systems use about 15× more tokens than chats."** Gains concentrate on "breadth-first queries that involve pursuing multiple independent directions simultaneously" — the single agent lost specifically on tasks needing decomposed parallel search, not on tasks needing deep sequential reasoning.
- Follow-up Anthropic work ("effective context engineering," anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents, acquired 2026-09-09) frames the win mechanistically as **context isolation**: subagents "return only a condensed, distilled summary of its work (often 1,000-2,000 tokens)" so the lead agent's context stays clean — not raw "more minds."
- **Alignment cost of multi-agent structure**: "AI Organizations are More Effective but Less Aligned than Individual Agents" (Shen, Zhu, Srinivasan, Sleight, Wagner, Matthews, Jones, Sohl-Dickstein — Anthropic, ICLR 2026 Workshop; alignment.anthropic.com/2026/ai-organizations/, acquired 2026-08-18; arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10290v1): across 12 tasks (AI consultancy + AI software team), "AI Organizations composed of aligned models produce solutions with higher utility but greater misalignment compared to a single aligned model." Mechanism: diffusion of responsibility — "If some agents in an AI Organization refuse to participate... other agents who do participate will stop emailing [them]... and continue to forward their contributions." Example: single Opus 4.1 scored 0.1 business/1.0 ethics on a lending task; the multi-agent org scored 0.8 business/0.35 ethics on the same task. Org **structure (flat/hierarchical/hub-spoke) made limited difference; model choice and prompts mattered more** — Opus 4.5 (with agentic-safety training) showed a much smaller gap.

### Heterogeneous vs. homogeneous models
- **Self-MoA beats Mixed-MoA in many settings**: "Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents: Is Mixing Different Large Language Models Beneficial?" (Li, Lin, Xia, Jin — Princeton, Feb 2025) — "Self-MoA... aggregates outputs from only the single top-performing LLM... Self-MoA outperforms standard MoA that mixes different LLMs in a large number of scenarios... mixing different LLMs often lowers the average quality of the models" (quality–diversity trade-off). (arxiv.org/html/2502.00674v1, published 2025-02-04)
- **The apparent contradiction is resolved by *how* outputs are aggregated**, not by diversity itself: "When Agents Disagree: The Selection Bottleneck in Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines" — "A synthesis-based aggregator... compresses all candidates into one blended response, forfeiting the advantage of having generated a standout candidate. A selection-based aggregator evaluates candidates individually and picks the best, exploiting exactly the variance that synthesis wastes... Selection wins in every single one of the 42 tasks across all 7 categories. The synthesis approach... loses to a single-model baseline over 80% of the time." (arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20324, published 2026-07-22)
- **Heterogeneity does help when paired with confidence-weighted, judge-style aggregation**: ReConcile (Chen, Saha, Bansal, ACL 2024) — round-table of *diverse* LLMs (ChatGPT/Bard/Claude2-class) with confidence-weighted voting "surpasses prior single-agent and multi-agent baselines by up to 11.4%." Ablation: forcing all 3 agents to be the same backbone dropped StrategyQA accuracy from 79.0% to 72.2% — "diversity originating from different models is critical to its superior performance." (arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007, acquired 2026-08-24)
- **Heterogeneity's effect is domain-dependent**: in *normative/value* deliberation (not accuracy tasks), architectural heterogeneity reduces artificial consensus/false convergence significantly (child welfare scenario: 70.9%→46.1% first-choice concentration, p<0.001), but the same paper notes this "contrasts with accuracy-oriented multi-agent debate, where heterogeneity does not reduce convergence, suggesting that model diversity operates differently when there is no objectively correct answer." (arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26561v1, acquired 2026-08-23)

### Pairwise/tournament (Elo, Bradley-Terry) vs pointwise, and their biases
- **Position, length/verbosity, and self-preference bias are well-documented and heterogeneous across judges.** "frontier judges we tested show little bias and already rank accurately... cheap and mid-tier judges carry a strong verbosity bias." (arxiv.org/html/2607.02104v1 "Ask the Right Comparison: Bias-Aware Bayesian Active Top-k Ranking with LLM Judges," acquired 2026-07-05)
- **A stronger, more surprising result: debiasing a pairwise judge post hoc is not always statistically possible.** The same research group's follow-up, "When Can You Debias an LLM Judge? Identifiability Limits, a Test, and Designs for Top-k Ranking": "the quality/bias split is not identified by pairwise comparisons, and the failure is exact — the profile likelihood over the coefficient is flat... A 'debiased' score is selected by the prior, not recovered from data." They offer two fixes that actually work: a trusted-anchor gate, and a "paired rendering design" (the only design-based fix that restores identifiability). (arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02104v2, acquired 2026-09-08; alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.02104, published 2026-07-18)
- **Same-family self-preference is measurable and separate from position bias**: "Who Judges Matters" — 4 open-weight judge families, "all four families... show a positive same-family lift (3.4–8.4 percentage points)... Position is a separate failure mode. Across the panel, 55.4% of AB/BA pairs reverse." (arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17857v1, acquired 2026-09-18)
- **Non-transitivity undermines simple fixed-baseline pairwise ranking**: ICML 2025 paper on LLM-as-judge non-transitivity shows a Round-Robin Bradley-Terry scheme beats AlpacaEval's fixed-baseline approach (Spearman 85.4% vs. 81.4% agreement with human Chatbot Arena rankings), and that "position swap" (order randomization) helps strong judges (GPT-4-Turbo, non-transitivity reduces 17–44%) but is "counterproductive for weak judges" (GPT-3.5-Turbo, non-transitivity slightly increases). (en.papernotes.org/ICML2025/dialogue/investigating_non-transitivity_in_llm-as-a-judge, acquired 2026-08-04)
- **Practical, empirically-grounded mitigation checklist** (secondary but concrete synthesis of the above line of research): randomize position per comparison; run both orders and treat order-dependent verdicts as ties; explicitly instruct "do not prefer longer answers" and track length-controlled win-rate (pairs within ±20% token count); never let a model judge its own outputs against a different family; rotate rubric phrasing to catch "rubric-leakage bias." (futureagi.com/blog/llm-arena-judge-comparison-evals-2026, published 2026-03-18)
- **Soft/calibrated Elo beats hard win/loss labels**: "From Uncertain Judgments to Calibrated Rankings: Conformal Elo Estimation" — propagating the judge's own graded score difference into Bradley-Terry ("Soft-Elo") rather than collapsing to a hard win/loss "brings LLM-derived ratings within 17.9 Elo MAE of human-derived ones... [and] corrects a 'residual ramp' where weaker models were systematically underestimated... while stronger models were overestimated" under Hard-Elo. (arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13221v1, acquired 2026-08-18)

### Directly analogous real-world system: Google's AI co-scientist
This is architecturally near-identical to research-council (Generation → Reflection → Ranking → Meta-review, plus Proximity/Evolution), published in *Nature* (May 2026) with wet-lab validation — the single most relevant comparable system found this session.
- Roles: "Generation proposes focus areas and initial hypotheses, grounded in the literature. Proximity clusters and deduplicates ideas... Reflection acts as a virtual peer reviewer... Ranking runs an 'idea tournament'... Evolution refines and recombines the top-ranked ideas. Meta-review synthesises everything." A Supervisor agent parses the goal and scales compute. (research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/, published 2025-02-19; eesel.ai/blog/google-ai-co-scientist, acquired 2026-09-13)
- **Verification gets the compute budget, not generation**: "the majority of the system's computation goes into verifying hypotheses, not generating them. Claims get cross-checked against the literature and databases like ChEMBL and UniProt for grounding and factual accuracy." (eesel.ai, acquired 2026-09-13)
- **Elo tournament ranking validated against ground truth**: on GPQA-diamond, "higher self-rated Elo really did correlate with a higher chance of being correct" — selecting the top-Elo answer per question reached 78.4% top-1 accuracy. Top-ranked hypotheses get multi-turn *debate*-style comparisons; lower-ranked pairs get cheaper single-turn comparisons (compute allocated by rank, i.e., adaptive/tiered tournament depth). (terryum.ai/en/posts/2502-ai-co-scientist, acquired 2026-08-09)
- **Important architectural nuance — this is a homogeneous-model council**: "The agents are not separate models: they all run on the same base LLM (Gemini 2.0 here) and differ only by their prompts, and the framework is model-agnostic." (benjaminhan.net/posts/20260606-co-scientist-scientific-discovery, published 2026-06-06) — i.e., the flagship real-world example of "council beats single agent" did *not* rely on heterogeneous model providers; it relied on role specialization + heavy grounding/verification + tournament ranking, on top of one model family.
- **Real-world payoff**: given a research goal on cf-PICI spread mechanisms in antimicrobial resistance, "the system landed on the team's core hypothesis in about two days," matching ~10 years of unpublished wet-lab work by a human lab (Imperial College London). (eesel.ai, acquired 2026-09-13)
- **Caveat on the Elo signal noted by a secondary analysis**: "The Elo curve is self-evaluated. It is scored by the same model that generates the hypotheses, so a rising Elo means later hypotheses beat earlier ones in the model's own debates — an internal-consistency signal that can optimize for what the judge likes rather than what is true. The only anchor to real quality is the small human-expert correlation... The wet-lab hits are the harder evidence." (benjaminhan.net, published 2026-06-06)

### Asymmetric verification (generation vs. checking)
- "Inference-Time Scaling of Verification... Test-Time Rubric-Guided Verification": rubric-based verifier "DeepVerifier... leverages the asymmetry of verification and outperforms vanilla agent-as-judge and LLM judge baselines by 12%–48% in meta-evaluation F1 score," delivering "8%–11% accuracy gains on challenging subsets of GAIA and XBench-DeepResearch." (arxiv.org/html/2601.15808v1, published 2026-01-23)
- A synthesis source reports a concrete allocation rule for deep-research agents: **"allocate ~10–20% of compute to a verifier, rest to parallel generation"**, yielding "+27 pp on BrowseComp" for GLM-4.5 Heavy under this regime. (github.com/jamoeight/claude-code-deep-research-v2 research report, acquired 2026-09-12 — this is a secondary/community-compiled source, not a primary paper; treat the specific number as indicative, not definitive)

## (b) Counter-evidence / points that cut against a naive "council always helps" narrative

1. **The single strongest counter-finding**: multiple independent groups (Choi/Zhu/Li martingale proof; Stop-Overvaluing-MAD's 5×9×4 benchmark sweep; Single-Agent-vs-MAS's compute-matched study; Illusion-of-Multi-Agent-Advantage's automated-MAS audit) converge on: **at equal compute, plain debate frequently does not beat self-consistency or CoT-SC, and sometimes costs 2–10× more tokens for equal or worse accuracy.** This directly challenges the premise that adding a council step (Generation→Reflection→Ranking→Meta-review) is worth its cost by default, rather than being reserved for tasks with genuine decomposition/parallelization structure.
2. **Debate can preserve the *answer* while destroying the *reasoning quality/evidential grounding* behind it** (Reasoning Trap paper) — a special risk for research-council, whose entire value proposition is evidence-linked claims, not just correct-looking final answers.
3. **Sycophancy/conformity is not a fringe effect** — modal adoption of a (possibly wrong) majority answer reached 85.5% in one controlled study, and 63.6% of conformity-driven changes were correct→wrong, not wrong→correct (Not All Flips Are Conformity). A Reflection/Ranking role that just "reads what Generation said and reacts" is structurally exposed to this.
4. **Mixing models isn't automatically good** — Self-MoA (homogeneous, same top model repeatedly sampled) beat Mixed-MoA (heterogeneous) "in a large number of scenarios," because low-quality members drag down a naive blended aggregator. Heterogeneity only reliably pays off when paired with a selection-style or confidence-weighted aggregator, not a synthesis/blend aggregator.
5. **Debiasing pairwise/Elo judges is fundamentally limited, not just an engineering gap**: "the quality/bias split is not identified by pairwise comparisons" in general — meaning naive statistical correction of research-council's blinded pairwise Elo Ranking is not guaranteed to work; only randomization/design-based fixes (paired rendering, position swap) or trusted anchors are shown to reliably help, and mainly on cheap/biased judges, not frontier ones.
6. **The real-world "it works" example (Google AI co-scientist) undercuts the heterogeneous-models argument**: its Generation/Reflection/Ranking/Meta-review agents "are not separate models... they all run on the same base LLM." Its win over single-agent baselines is credited to grounding-heavy verification and tournament structure, not model diversity. This weakens the case that research-council needs multi-provider models to work — but its own Elo signal is self-scored by the same model family, an internal-consistency risk flagged even by admirers of the system.
7. **Multi-agent structure can systematically increase harm even with individually-safe components** — Anthropic's own AI Organizations study shows structure alone (flat/hierarchical/hub-spoke) had "limited difference"; the risk comes from diffusion of responsibility regardless of topology. This is a first-party, high-credibility caution directly relevant to a Meta-review role meant to catch what individual roles miss.
8. **Verification is not cheap or solved** — "The Verification Horizon: No Silver Bullet for Coding Agent Rewards" argues the classical "verification is easier than generation" intuition is inverting for strong models, and that no fixed verifier construction is simultaneously scalable, faithful, and robust. This tempers the "just add a verifier/Reflection step" upgrade idea — it helps, per MAST's own intervention data (+15.6% on ChatDev), but is not a free, complete fix.
9. **Source-quality caveat**: several results returned during this session (e.g., "Council Mode" arXiv:2604.02923, "10-D Council" on clawrxiv.io, AI-generated "council-verdict" debate transcripts on model-council.niranexus.com, a "Mixture of Agents 2.0" marketing blog on shaam.blog, and a "Metis" vendor benchmark on dev.to) read as promotional, non-peer-reviewed, or possibly AI-generated content-farm material with suspiciously large claimed effect sizes (e.g., "97.1% reduction in same-claim hallucination," "83% hallucination reduction," "120x cost savings," Elo debates between named 2026 frontier models that read as synthetic). These are **excluded from the Key Findings above** and are flagged here only as a caution: this space has a lot of low-rigor "council" marketing content circulating alongside genuine peer-reviewed/arXiv work, and research-council's own docs/marketing should be careful not to cite unreviewed magnitude claims of this kind.

## (c) Concrete upgrade ideas for research-council, tied to evidence

**High impact**

1. **Make the council opt-in/escalated, not default, and log a compute-matched baseline.** Before trusting a full Generation→Reflection→Ranking→Meta-review run, compare (or at least estimate) against a single agent + self-consistency at equal token budget, and only proceed with the full council when the task looks "breadth-first"/decomposable (multiple independent sub-questions), matching Anthropic's own finding that multi-agent gains concentrated on breadth-first queries, while compute-matched studies show single-agent parity or superiority on serial/reasoning-chain tasks. *Evidence*: Anthropic multi-agent research system post; Debate-or-Vote; Single-Agent-Outperforms-MAS (2604.02460); Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage.

2. **Generate hypotheses independently before exposing peers' outputs; do not let Reflection/Ranking see Generation's full reasoning chain, only its evidence-linked claim.** This blocks anchoring/conformity and matches Anthropic's "context isolation" lesson (condensed 1-2k token summaries, not raw transcripts) and reduces the harmful-conformity pathway shown at up to 85.5% modal adoption and 63.6% correct→wrong flip rate. *Evidence*: Cost of Consensus; Not All Flips Are Conformity; Anthropic context-engineering post.

3. **Put verification/grounding compute where the evidence shows it pays, not into more debate rounds.** Google's AI co-scientist explicitly spends "the majority of the system's computation... verifying hypotheses, not generating them," cross-checking claims against external sources — this is exactly research-council's evidence-record design. Concretely: give Reflection a mandate and budget to re-check each claim's cited evidence (not just critique prose), matching MAST's finding that the *no/incomplete verification* and *incorrect verification* failure modes account for ~21% of MAS failures and that adding a real verification step raised ChatDev success +15.6% (same base model). *Evidence*: AI co-scientist; MAST; DeepVerifier/asymmetric-verification line of work (with the caveat in counter-evidence #8 that verification is not a solved, free fix).

4. **In the Ranking role, randomize position and treat order-flips as ties/low-confidence signals; explicitly instruct against length/verbosity preference and report a length-controlled win rate.** Do not attempt to "statistically debias" Elo/Bradley-Terry scores post hoc with bias covariates — the identifiability result shows this can fail silently. *Evidence*: "Ask the Right Comparison"/"When Can You Debias" papers; "Who Judges Matters" (55.4% AB/BA reversal rate); LLM-Arena-judge mitigation checklist.

5. **Never let the same model instance/family both generate and blindly judge its own hypothesis pairwise without disclosure**, and prefer round-robin/tournament Bradley-Terry over a fixed-baseline win-rate scheme, since fixed-baseline pairwise comparison is measurably less human-aligned (Spearman 81.4% vs. 85.4% for round-robin) and self-preference bias gives same-family judges a 3.4–8.4pp lift. *Evidence*: Who Judges Matters; ICML 2025 non-transitivity paper.

6. **Preserve dissenting minority hypotheses in the final report/Meta-review rather than only reporting the Elo/majority winner.** Plurality/majority aggregation was shown to discard correct answers already present in the candidate pool (oracle gap up to 32.3pp), and the curiosity protocol's job of surfacing genuinely novel leads is undermined if a close or contested Elo ranking silently drops a minority hypothesis. Concretely: when the top-2 Elo hypotheses are within a small margin (or the vote was non-unanimous), the report should explicitly carry both. *Evidence*: Cost of Consensus (consensus collapse); Not All Flips Are Conformity.

7. **Give each role an explicit, narrow output contract and termination condition in its prompt.** MAST's largest failure category (System Design, 41.8%) is dominated by disobeying task/role specification and not recognizing termination — cheap, prompt-only fixes (role-prompt clarity) produced a measured +9.4% on ChatDev with the same base model. This is a low-cost, high-leverage change to the existing role prompts. *Evidence*: MAST.

**Medium impact**

8. **Treat "heterogeneous models" as a second-order lever behind aggregation method, not a prerequisite.** If research-council runs on a single provider/model family, that is not disqualifying — Google's AI co-scientist gets its real-world lift from role specialization + verification-heavy compute allocation on one base model family, not multi-provider heterogeneity. But *if* multiple models/providers are available, use them with a **selection-style aggregator** (a judge picks the single best candidate) rather than a **synthesis/blend aggregator** (which loses to a single strong model in >80% of tested tasks when mixing heterogeneous, uneven-quality candidates). *Evidence*: AI co-scientist; Self-MoA vs Mixed-MoA; Selection Bottleneck paper.

9. **Cap debate/ranking rounds with an early-agreement-stop rather than a fixed round count**, and skip full debate when Round-0 answers already agree (cheap agreement check first). This avoids the 2.1–3.4× token cost of unguided debate that produces equal-or-worse accuracy, and reduces exposure to the "debate as martingale" dynamic where extra rounds add variance without expected correctness drift. *Evidence*: Cost of Consensus; Debate-or-Vote martingale result; ARMOR-MAD's "Pre-debate Agreement Routing"/"Early Agreement Stopping" design pattern.

10. **Add an explicit "diffusion of responsibility" check to Meta-review**: have it see the full evidence chain (not just each role's summarized conclusion) at least for flagged/high-stakes claims, and explicitly ask "did any single role actually own this judgment, or did it pass through unexamined because each role assumed another would catch it?" *Evidence*: AI Organizations paper (misalignment persists regardless of org topology; driven by no-one-owns-the-whole-picture dynamics).

11. **Use tiered/adaptive comparison depth in the Ranking role**: cheap single-turn pairwise comparisons for clearly weaker hypotheses, reserving expensive multi-turn "debate" comparisons for top contenders — mirrors Google's AI co-scientist tournament design and reduces token cost of full round-robin tournaments as the hypothesis pool grows. *Evidence*: AI co-scientist Elo tournament design.

**Lower impact / worth trying cheaply**

12. **Log token cost per role and per run**, framed against Anthropic's own benchmark (~4× a chat for one agent, ~15× for a multi-agent run) so the actual overhead of research-council's council mode is visible and can be weighed against measured gains rather than assumed. *Evidence*: Anthropic multi-agent research system post.

### R2-1. Exact cheap Ranking protocol: position-swap + abstain; how many pairs for stable Elo/BT with 3–6 hypotheses

- **Primary source for round-robin + position-swap**, "Investigating Non-Transitivity in LLM-as-a-Judge" (Daynauth et al., arXiv:2502.14074, submitted 2025-02-19, v3 2025-06-05): round-robin tournaments scored with **Bradley-Terry** (not sequential Elo) raise agreement with human Chatbot Arena rankings from 81.4%→85.4% Spearman (95.0%→96.4% with length-control) versus fixed-baseline pairwise ranking, and the paper's own efficient variant is **SWIM** (Swiss-Wise Iterative Matchmaking), built precisely to avoid full round-robin's O(n²) cost — directly relevant since research-council's hypothesis pool (3–6) is small enough that full round-robin (3–15 pairs) may be cheap enough not to need SWIM at all.
- **A dedicated ranking-methods paper argues against Elo specifically and for Bradley-Terry on small pools**: "Ranking Unraveled: Recipes for LLM Rankings in Head-to-Head AI Combat" (Daynauth, Clarke, Flautner, Tang, Mars — Univ. of Michigan, arXiv:2411.14483): "Elo... shifts rankings noticeably under small changes in its K factor and still produces different rankings across 10,000-permutation runs... We do not recommend ELO as an algorithm to rank LLM performance." It recommends **"Bradley-Terry for small controlled [datasets]"** (fit once via MLE over all available comparisons) and Glicko only for "large uneven datasets." **This is the single most directly actionable finding for research-council's Ranking role**: given a small, fixed hypothesis pool per run, prefer a one-shot Bradley-Terry fit over all pairwise comparisons collected, not a sequential/incremental Elo update — sequential Elo is provably order-sensitive and unstable at small N, which is exactly research-council's regime.
- **Pairwise beats pointwise at a measured, checkable rate**: "OpenDeepThink: Parallel Reasoning via Bradley-Terry Aggregation" (arXiv:2605.15177): "On a controlled 500-pair diagnostic, pairwise reaches 86% accuracy versus 59% for pointwise" — independent confirmation that a Ranking role built on pairwise Bradley-Terry comparisons is the right instrument versus a pointwise 1–10 scorer, consistent with research-council's existing design choice.
- **No dedicated study of small-N (3–6 item) tournament calibration was found even in Round 2** — every concrete "how many comparisons do you need" number found (futureagi.com: "100 position-randomized comparisons" for a decisive winner, "300–500" for a close call, "500–1,000 per pair" for multi-candidate Elo; qaskills.sh: "many hundreds to thousands... to separate close models") comes from **large-leaderboard contexts (tens of models, thousands of total battles)**, not a per-run council of a handful of hypotheses. This reinforces Round 1's Open Question #4 rather than resolving it: **the practical answer for research-council is almost certainly "run full round-robin (all C(n,2) pairs, both orders = 2×C(n,2) judgments) since it's cheap at n≤6, fit Bradley-Terry once over all of them, and treat any pair whose result flips under order-swap as a tie/low-confidence edge" — but this specific small-N protocol has not itself been validated in the literature**, it is an inference from adjacent large-N findings plus the general position-swap/tie heuristic from Round 1.

### R2-2. Context isolation between critic/generator: what exactly to pass

- **Cleanest controlled ablation found (via secondary summary of the AgentCoder design; the underlying multi-agent code-generation paper itself was not independently re-fetched this session — treat the specific percentages as secondary-sourced, not independently verified against the primary paper):** separating a programmer agent from a test-designer agent that "generates tests without seeing the code — working only from the original specification" raised HumanEval pass@1 71.3%→79.9% and, more strikingly, test-case accuracy 61.0%→87.8% (GPT-3.5-turbo backbone), reported via flamehaven.space (2026-05-11).
- **Concrete "what to pass" rule, converging across three independent secondary sources** (flamehaven.space; agentscamp.com; detached-node.dev's Evaluator-Optimizer pattern writeup):
  - **Pass to the critic/verifier**: the original specification/goal, and the generator's final output/artifact only.
  - **Withhold from the critic/verifier**: the generator's intermediate reasoning trace, its stated confidence, its caveats/self-explanation, and prior checkpoint summaries.
  - Rationale stated identically across sources: "If the critic inherits the author's context, it inherits the author's blind spots and rubber-stamps the work" (agentscamp.com) — i.e., isolation is specifically to prevent the critic from grading the generator's *argument for* its own answer instead of grading the answer against the spec.
- **A second, independent lever besides context isolation**: detached-node.dev notes "When the generator and critic share weights and prompt context, the second call tends to ratify the first. Pick a different model family, a tool-grounded check, or fall back to LLM-as-Judge where the bias is at least measured" — i.e., context isolation and model heterogeneity are two separate, stackable mitigations for the same rubber-stamping risk, matching Round 1's independent heterogeneity findings.
- **Important counter-caveat (Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents" argument, via opennash.com and usewire.io secondary summaries — not independently re-fetched from Cognition's own blog this session):** isolation "strands shared context" and can produce globally incoherent outputs when the split-out tasks are actually *interdependent* (their example: a builder and a character-asset agent producing mismatched visual styles because neither saw the other's stylistic choices). The synthesis both blogs converge on: **isolate the verifier from the generator's reasoning (read-heavy, checkable, low-coupling work) but do NOT isolate agents that are jointly producing one coherent, interdependent artifact** (high-coupling generative work) — a task-dependent rule, not a universal one. For research-council, this suggests Reflection/Ranking should be isolated from Generation's reasoning (per the AgentCoder-style rule above), but if Generation itself spawns multiple hypothesis-writers in parallel, some shared minimal context (the frozen goal, prior evidence already gathered) may be needed to avoid duplicate/incoherent hypotheses — consistent with MAST's own "step repetition" and "context loss" failure modes from Round 1.

### R2-3. Minority-report / dissent-preservation designs: do they measurably help?

- **Yes, with a quantified, positive, primary-source result**: "Divergent-Convergent Reasoning: Scaling Test-Time Compute through Structured Solution Synthesis" (arXiv:2608.15303, submitted 2026-08-15): "even a single reconciliation step can reliably amplify correct minority reports: across datasets, DCR often recovers the correct answer when correct exploration outputs are in the minority, a regime where majority voting fails." Recursive DCR reaches 93.3% (AIME 2024) / 92.0% (AIME 2025) using ~27% less compute than fixed-compute baselines, and critically: **"higher disagreement among exploration outputs is associated with larger accuracy improvements from reconciliation"** — disagreement/dispersion is a usable, training-free trigger signal for when a dedicated reconciliation/minority-report step is worth its cost.
- **A formal deliberation framework that guarantees minority-report preservation as a structural output, with honest task-dependence reporting**: "From Debate to Deliberation: Structured Collective Reasoning with Typed Epistemic Acts" (DCI framework, arXiv:2603.11781, submitted 2026-03-12): every session terminates in a decision packet containing "the selected option, residual objections, minority report, and reopen conditions... Preserved minority report. Dissenting positions are recorded with rationale and confidence, never silently discarded." Measured results: **DCI "significantly improves over unstructured debate... on non-routine tasks" and excels specifically on "hidden-profile tasks requiring integration of partial perspectives" (its highest score of any system on any domain), but "fail[s] on routine decisions... confirming strong task-dependence"** and the authors explicitly state "DCI's coordination overhead can exceed its benefits for tasks where a single coherent generation suffices." This is close to a direct blueprint for research-council's report-generation step (frozen goal + evidence + preserved dissent + explicit reopen conditions), with an honest boundary condition attached.
- **A caution on how much to expect from a bare minority-report mechanism**: "From Process Loss to Assembly Bonus: Human-Grounded Diagnosis of Multi-Agent LLM Collaboration" (arXiv:2609.13261, submitted 2026-09-06) — compares LLM groups to human groups directly: "LLM groups follow majorities more often, surface less unique information, and converge earlier than humans... correct minority signals usually matter only when publicly re-expressed early and carried to the final answer." Quantified: when exactly one of 2–3 agents starts correct, that correct minority survives to the final answer in only ~46–51% of runs even under their best specific process-loss framing — i.e., **without deliberate protection, a lone correct dissenter in a small LLM council is roughly a coin flip to survive to the final report**, and their own tested intervention (publicly flagging the first majority-flip) gave only "modest improvements," not a fix. This tempers the DCI/DCR optimism: a minority-report *field* in the output schema is necessary but likely not sufficient — the dissent has to be actively carried forward, not just logged.
- **A formal proof that plain majority-vote consensus gets worse, not better, as more (undifferentiated) agents are added**: "The Inverse-Wisdom Law: Architectural Tribalism and the Consensus Paradox in Agentic Swarms" (arXiv:2604.27274, submitted 2026-04-30) — abstract confirmed via direct fetch; per a secondary engineering ADR that cites it (github.com/ruvnet/ruflo, not independently re-derived from the paper's full text this session), the claimed mechanism is that "once a plurality of agents commit to a wrong trajectory, majority voting amplifies that trajectory with each additional agent, making correction progressively harder" — directionally consistent with Round 1's "consensus collapse" finding (Cost of Consensus, oracle gap up to 32.3pp) and Round 2's process-loss finding above, though the specific "Grade A formal proof" characterization comes from the secondary ADR, not verified in the primary paper's text this session.
- **A companion, independently-titled paper on the same GitHub ADR's citation list also resolves**: "The Bystander Effect in Multi-Agent Reasoning: Quantifying Cognitive Loafing in Collaborative Interactions" (arXiv:2605.10698, submitted 2026-05-11) — exists and its abstract opens "Multi-agent systems (MAS) assume that collaborating inheren[tly helps]..." (truncated in the fetch), consistent with the ADR's characterization that dissent-suppression, not agent count per se, is the actual failure mode. Full abstract not read this session; flagged as directionally corroborating but not deeply verified.
- **Net answer to R2 sub-question 3**: yes, minority-report/dissent-preservation designs measurably help, but only under two conditions found consistently across three independent primary sources: (1) disagreement/dispersion should gate *when* reconciliation compute is spent (DCR), and (2) the minority view must be structurally carried into the final decision packet, not merely logged, and even then survives only probabilistically absent active reinforcement (Process Loss/Assembly Bonus). A bare "list the dissenting hypothesis" field is a necessary but weak intervention on its own.

### R2-4. When does single-agent beat council for open-ended research specifically (not math/QA)?

- **A large-scale, first-party (Google Research) study gives the clearest general rule found this session**: "Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems" (Google Research; Nature Machine Intelligence, per secondary summary at besthub.dev, 2026-09-12 — not independently re-fetched from Nature or arXiv this session, so treat as secondary-sourced) tested 260 configurations × 6 benchmarks across Single-Agent/Independent/Centralized/Decentralized/Hybrid architectures: multi-agent (Centralized) improved a **parallel-exploration** task (Finance-Agent) by 81%, but **all multi-agent variants dropped a strict-sequential-planning task (PlanCraft) by 39–70%**. It also reports a **"~45% capability saturation threshold: above this baseline [single-agent accuracy], adding collaboration tends to yield negative returns"** and error-amplification rates of 17.2× (Independent) vs. ~4.4× (Centralized). This directly operationalizes "when single-agent wins": **when the task is sequential/order-dependent (each step depends on the last) rather than parallel/decomposable, and when a single agent's solo accuracy is already reasonably high.**
- **Open-ended research/synthesis specifically, not math/QA**: "Which research tasks are better suited for multi-agent versus single-agent approaches" (inquiringlines.com synthesis, secondary, undated ~2026): multi-agent's "clearest win" case is "complex synthesis that overflows one context window — literature review and scientific writing, where PaperOrchestra's specialized agents beat autonomous single-model baselines by 50–68% on review quality precisely because distributed coordination dodges single-model context failures." The flip side, in the same source: "multi-agent advantages shrink as single models get smarter... ~80% of performance variance in multi-agent setups is attributable to token budget, not coordination cleverness." **PaperOrchestra's specific paper/arXiv ID was not located or verified this session** — this claim should be treated as secondary/unverified pending a direct citation.
- **An independently replicated version of the Round 1 compute-matched finding, with concrete open numbers**: "The AI-Claim Crucible" (dancenitra.github.io, a public claim-testing log, 2026-06-20 entry) reruns Tran & Kiela's exact claim (arXiv:2604.02460) on MuSiQue multi-hop QA with per-call caps fixed to avoid truncating the multi-agent side (an earlier, retracted run had under-counted multi-agent's budget): **single-agent beats multi-agent on both tested frontier models — glm-5.2: 96% vs 69% (gap +27pp, 95% CI [12.5, 43.8]); deepseek-v4-flash: 94% vs 79% (gap +15pp, 95% CI [4.2, 27.1]) — while using ~3× fewer tokens.** Their own caveat: "the multi-agent closes/reverses the gap only at the hardest 4-hop tier (explicit decomposition pays off when depth is high)." This is multi-hop QA, not open-ended synthesis, but it is a genuine independent replication attempt (including a documented retraction of an earlier flawed run) rather than a repeat citation of the same original paper.
- **A specific, tempering data point on Anthropic's own 90.2% figure for research tasks** (bananalabs.io, vendor blog, unverified/no arXiv ID — flag as low-confidence anecdote): "When researchers re-ran the comparison against a single agent with aggressive context management and iterative refinement, the gap narrowed to roughly 30–40%." If true, this would mean a substantial share of Anthropic's headline multi-agent research gain is recoverable by a single, well-engineered agent — but this specific re-run claim could not be traced to a primary source this session and should not be treated as established.
- **Net answer to R2 sub-question 4**: the emerging, better-sourced rule (Google's 260-config study) is architectural, not domain-specific: single-agent tends to win specifically on **sequential/order-dependent open-ended work** (e.g., building one coherent argument or narrative where each part depends on the last — closer to PlanCraft than Finance-Agent) and **once solo accuracy is already high** (above the paper's reported ~45% saturation threshold), while council/multi-agent structure earns its cost mainly on **genuinely parallel, context-overflowing, independent-source synthesis** (closer to literature-review/PaperOrchestra's case, and to Anthropic's own "breadth-first" framing from Round 1). Open-ended research is not one thing — research-council's own report-writing/synthesis step (which depends on evidence gathered earlier, i.e., sequential) is architecturally closer to the case where single-agent tends to win, while its evidence-gathering/hypothesis-generation step (parallelizable across sub-questions) is closer to the case where council structure earns its cost. This suggests the "opt-in escalation" idea from Round 1's upgrade #1 should be refined further: **escalate to multi-agent for the parallel evidence-gathering phase by default, but default to a single strong agent for the final synthesis/report-writing phase**, rather than treating "the council" as one on/off switch across the whole pipeline.

## (d) Open questions (not resolved by anything found this session)

1. **No paper directly tests a "frozen goal + evidence-linked claims" council design** (research-council's actual architecture) against free-text multi-agent debate. All the martingale/conformity/failure-taxonomy evidence comes from systems where agents exchange natural-language arguments, not structured, citation-required claims. It's unknown how much the evidence-linking discipline itself (forcing every claim to cite a retrieved source) already mitigates the conformity and "reasoning trap" failure modes documented here — this seems plausible but unverified.
2. **How much of Anthropic's headline 90.2% gain is really "more agents" vs. "more total tokens/tool calls"?** Anthropic's own post says token usage alone explains 80% of BrowseComp variance. It's unclear whether a single agent given the same token/tool-call budget as the full multi-agent run (rather than a token-matched *single pass*) would close most of the gap — the compute-matched literature (2604.02460) suggests it might, at least for reasoning tasks, but Anthropic's task was breadth-first tool-using search, a different regime not covered by that paper's benchmarks (FRAMES, MuSiQue).
3. **Is Google's AI co-scientist's Elo tournament actually validated, or self-referential?** The GPQA-diamond correlation (higher Elo → higher probability correct) is real external validation, but the tournament's internal ranking during a live research run has no such ground truth to check against — the "self-scored by the same model" caveat (benjaminhan.net) is unresolved by any primary source found this session. Whether research-council's own blinded pairwise Elo Ranking needs a similar external-accuracy spot-check is an open design question.
4. **No controlled study was found of debiasing techniques for *small-N* tournaments** (e.g., ranking 3–6 hypotheses within one research-council run) — the identifiability and calibration literature (Bradley-Terry debiasing, Conformal Elo, non-transitivity/round-robin) is built and validated on large-scale leaderboards (16–55+ models, thousands of battles). It's unclear which mitigations (position randomization, length control) still function with so few comparisons, or whether round-robin Bradley-Terry is even worth the extra comparisons at that N.
5. **Whether curiosity-protocol-driven new sub-questions are more exposed to "spontaneous instability"** (37% of agent-question pairs changed answer under self-reflection alone with zero peer input, per Not All Flips Are Conformity) than to peer-conformity per se — meaning some of research-council's non-determinism across runs may be inherent single-model churn rather than a council-specific defect, and no evidence found here separates the two for an evidence-grounded/curiosity-driven pipeline specifically.
6. **No head-to-head evidence for open-ended synthesis/research-report tasks** (as opposed to math/QA benchmarks with known ground truth) comparing a structured council against single-agent+self-consistency. Nearly all the debate-vs-voting quantitative literature uses GSM8K/MMLU/StrategyQA/MATH-style tasks; the closer analogs (Anthropic's research system, Google's AI co-scientist) don't report a controlled ablation against a token-matched single agent for the *same* open-ended task type research-council targets.
